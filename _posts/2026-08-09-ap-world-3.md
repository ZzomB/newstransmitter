## 📖 Main Content

### A Regional Assault
Over the weekend, Typhoon Dolphin demonstrated the vast, transboundary reach of modern tropical storms, leaving a trail of disruption and tragedy across the Philippines, Taiwan, and mainland China. As the storm moved across the region, it forced governments to choose between massive economic pauses—such as grounding entire aviation networks—and risking catastrophic loss of life.

### China’s Eastern Seaboard Braces for Impact
Typhoon Dolphin officially made landfall on Sunday evening, striking near the city of Taizhou in Zhejiang province and brushing the northern coast of neighboring Fujian province. At the time of landfall, the storm was generating sustained winds of 151 kph (93 mph), whipped up massive coastal waves, and carried the threat of extreme rainfall. Forecasters warned that affected areas could see between 200 and 400 millimeters (7.9 to 15.7 inches) of rain over the subsequent days, raising alarms over flash floods and mudslides.

To mitigate the disaster, Chinese authorities launched a massive civil defense operation:
* **Evacuations:** In Taizhou alone, some 390,000 people were moved to safer ground, while Shanghai evacuated over 30,000 residents from high-risk zones.
* **Travel Shutdowns:** Maritime authorities suspended ferries and cruises in Zhejiang.
* **Aviation Gridlock:** Shanghai’s two primary air hubs, Pudong and Hongqiao, canceled approximately 60% of Sunday's scheduled flights—amounting to over 1,300 cancellations—severely disrupting domestic and international travel.

### The Collateral Crisis in the Philippines
While eastern China prepared for a direct hit, the Philippines was already grappling with the storm's indirect, yet devastating, consequences. Typhoon Dolphin acted as a massive atmospheric engine, pulling in and intensifying seasonal monsoon rains over the northern island of Luzon.

This "supercharged" monsoon triggered a devastating landslide in the mountain resort city of Baguio on Sunday, burying three homes. Emergency workers managed to rescue five survivors, but at least seven people remained missing. The disaster followed a week of already-saturated soils caused by a preceding tropical storm, which had already claimed six lives in the Benguet and Rizal provinces.

In total, the resulting floods displaced more than 12,000 residents in Luzon and Metropolitan Manila, forcing municipal governments to shut down schools and public services.

### Taiwan Feels the Outer Bands
Before slamming into the Chinese mainland, Dolphin's outer bands brushed northern Taiwan on Saturday. The capital city of Taipei saw heavy downpours, and several Taiwanese carriers canceled flights bound for mainland China on Sunday as safety precautions.

## 💡 Key Takeaways & Analysis

### 1. The Power of Preemptive Mobilization
China’s response to Typhoon Dolphin highlights how state-led, large-scale preemptive evacuations have become a standard, highly effective tool in mitigating natural disasters. Safely moving over 400,000 people in a matter of 24 to 48 hours requires sophisticated logistical planning and high levels of civic compliance. While the economic cost of shutting down transport networks and evacuating cities is immense, the low casualty rates in China during such major landfalls demonstrate that prioritizing human life over short-term economic activity pays off.

### 2. The Danger of "Indirect" Storm Impacts
The tragedy in the Philippines serves as a stark reminder that a typhoon’s danger zone extends far beyond its eye. Even without making direct landfall, Dolphin's ability to siphon moisture and accelerate regional monsoon systems caused catastrophic flooding and landslides hundreds of miles away. Meteorological agencies and disaster response teams must increasingly communicate these "indirect" risks to the public, as communities well outside the projected cone of a typhoon can still face life-threatening weather.

### 3. Economic Vulnerability of Coastal Megacities
Shanghai’s decision to cancel over 1,300 flights—60% of its daily schedule—underscores the acute vulnerability of global economic hubs to climate events. Shanghai is not just a major population center; it is a vital node in global supply chains and business travel. As intense storms become more frequent due to rising sea temperatures, the economic ripple effects of halting operations at major logistics and aviation hubs will continue to challenge global markets and domestic economies alike.
